MANUFACTURING MACHINE PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE SOP

Purpose:
This procedure provides general guidance for preventive maintenance of industrial manufacturing equipment.

MAINTENANCE SCHEDULING

Machines should be inspected according to operating hours, manufacturer recommendations, machine condition, and maintenance history.

Machines with long intervals since their previous maintenance should receive priority for inspection.

VIBRATION INSPECTION

Abnormal vibration can indicate bearing wear, imbalance, misalignment, looseness, lubrication problems, or other mechanical issues.

When elevated vibration is detected:

- Inspect bearings.
- Check shaft alignment.
- Inspect couplings.
- Check mounting and foundation bolts.
- Verify lubrication condition.
- Review recent vibration trends.

TEMPERATURE INSPECTION

Elevated machine temperature can indicate:

- Insufficient cooling.
- Bearing problems.
- Excessive friction.
- Lubrication problems.
- Electrical or motor-related issues.

When abnormal temperature is detected:

- Inspect cooling systems.
- Check lubrication.
- Inspect bearings.
- Check motor condition.
- Compare temperature with historical operating values.

HIGH OPERATING HOURS

Machines with high accumulated operating hours should receive additional inspection attention.

Inspect wear-prone components including:

- Bearings.
- Belts.
- Couplings.
- Gears.
- Shafts.
- Lubrication systems.

FAILURE HISTORY

Machines with repeated previous failures should be reviewed for recurring failure patterns.

Maintenance teams should examine previous maintenance records and identify whether corrective actions addressed the root cause.

MAINTENANCE DOCUMENTATION

Every maintenance activity should record:

- Machine identifier.
- Date.
- Operating hours.
- Observed condition.
- Components inspected.
- Components replaced.
- Corrective actions.
- Technician observations.
